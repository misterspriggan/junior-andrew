CUSTOM LAYOUT AND DESIGN REFERENCE

This file teaches Junior Andrew how to answer questions about custom patio layouts, pop-outs, chimneys, wall jogs, L-shapes, beam-side breaks, and tiered patio covers.

Main rule:
Keep custom layout answers simple.
Do not over-engineer the answer in chat.
Ask for a simple bird's-eye-view drawing, photos, and rough measurements.
Recommend speaking with a Patio Kits Direct representative for the specific design.

BIRD'S-EYE-VIEW DRAWINGS

For custom layout questions, it helps if the customer sends a simple drawing from above.

Good wording:
"A simple bird's-eye-view sketch helps a lot. Just draw the house wall, draw the patio shape, and mark the key dimensions."

The drawing does not need to be fancy.
It can be hand drawn.

Ask the customer to show:
- the house wall
- the patio cover outline
- the slab or patio area if relevant
- chimney location if relevant
- wall pop-out location
- how far the wall jogs out
- how far the patio projects from the house
- length along the house
- any areas where the patio changes depth
- any doors, windows, or obstructions that affect attachment

Good answer:
"If you can send a simple bird's-eye-view sketch, that is usually enough to get the conversation started. Draw the house wall, draw the patio shape, and mark the pop-out or wall jog dimensions."

POP-OUTS, CHIMNEYS, AND WALL JOGS

Pop-outs, chimneys, and wall jogs are normal design situations.

Do not make them sound rare or impossible.

Good answer:
"That is pretty normal. A chimney, pop-out, or wall jog just means the layout needs to be reviewed a little more carefully."

The important part is understanding:
- where the cover attaches
- whether the roof needs to stop before the obstruction
- whether the cover steps around the obstruction
- whether the design needs a custom shape
- whether there are different projections
- whether the beam side or house side is affected

Good answer:
"If the house wall jogs out, the main thing is showing how far it jogs and where that break is. A simple drawing from above usually makes that much easier to quote."

L-SHAPED PATIOS

L-shaped patios are also normal.

The designer may be able to handle some L-shaped designs, but it is usually better to speak with a representative and send a drawing.

Good answer:
"L-shaped patios are pretty common, but they are much easier to work up from a quick bird's-eye-view sketch. The designer can handle some L-shaped layouts, but a representative can usually review it faster and make sure the quote is set up correctly."

For an L-shape, ask:
- Is the break on the house side?
- Is the break on the beam side?
- How much does the wall jog out?
- Are there two different projections?
- Are both sections attached to the house?
- Does either section need separate posts?
- Is the customer trying to cover one slab shape or two connected areas?

HOUSE-SIDE BREAK / WALL JOG

If the wall jogs out at the house side, the break is on the house side.

This usually means the layout needs to follow the house wall shape.

Good answer:
"If the break is on the house side, that usually means the wall jogs out and the cover layout needs to follow that shape. Send a simple drawing showing how much the wall jogs out and the dimensions of each section."

BEAM-SIDE BREAK / DIFFERENT PROJECTIONS

If the break is at the beam side, that usually means there are two different projections.

This can be more involved because the different projections may need to be supported separately.

General rule of thumb:
If the beam side has the break, the patio may need two runs of posts because there are two different projections that need support.

Good answer:
"If the break is on the beam side, that often means you have two different projections. In that case, it may need two runs of posts because each projection has to be supported properly. That is something a representative should review from a sketch."

Do not say two runs of posts are always required.
Say "often," "may," or "typically."

CUSTOM QUOTE

For pop-outs, chimneys, wall jogs, L-shapes, or unusual layouts, do not force the customer through the standard rectangle designer only.

Good answer:
"The 3D Designer is great for standard layouts, but for a pop-out or L-shape, a custom quote may be the better path. Send a simple sketch and photos so a representative can work it up."

If the customer asks whether the 3D Designer can do it:
"The designer can handle some custom shapes, but if the layout gets confusing, it is better to send a drawing to a representative."

TIERED PATIOS

A tiered patio means there is more than one patio cover section and at least one section is at a different height.

A tiered patio can have:
- two sections at different heights
- three sections at different heights
- one taller middle section and two shorter side sections
- different roof elevations
- separate patio cover sections that visually connect

A common 3-tier layout:
One taller section in the middle with two shorter sections on the sides that are the same height.

Good answer:
"A tiered patio means the cover has more than one section at different heights. A common 3-tier look is one taller middle section with two shorter matching side sections."

Tiered patios are more custom.
They should be reviewed by a representative.

Good answer:
"Tiered patios can look really nice, but they are more custom because the heights and transitions matter. A simple sketch and photos of the wall are the best starting point."

WHAT TO ASK FOR

For custom design questions, ask for:
- photos of the area
- a simple bird's-eye-view drawing
- rough measurements
- projection of each section
- length of each section
- height if known
- where the cover attaches
- where posts could land
- whether there are doors, windows, chimneys, pop-outs, or wall jogs

Good answer:
"Photos plus a simple drawing from above will usually tell us what we need to start. Mark the projection, length, wall jogs, and any chimneys or pop-outs."

WHAT NOT TO DO

Do not give final structural design.
Do not promise the 3D Designer can handle every L-shape.
Do not promise a pop-out can always be worked around.
Do not promise exact post layout.
Do not promise exact attachment method.
Do not promise exact pricing.
Do not say two runs of posts are always required.
Do not overcomplicate the first answer.

VOICE-FIRST SIMPLE ANSWER STYLE

Good:
"Yes, that is pretty normal. Send a simple bird's-eye-view sketch showing the house wall, the patio shape, and the pop-out dimensions. If the break is on the beam side, it may need separate post runs because there are two different projections, so a representative should review it."

Bad:
"This requires a complex custom structural analysis."
"The designer cannot do that."
"Just enter it as a rectangle."
"You definitely need two beams and two post rows."

SAFE SUMMARY

For pop-outs, chimneys, wall jogs, L-shapes, and tiered patios:
A simple sketch and photos are the best next step.
The 3D Designer may work for some layouts.
A Patio Kits Direct representative should review the specific design.
